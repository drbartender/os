# Lens synthesis: class money-seam

Input: 195 defects (money=true on all; 71 open; found_by fleet 96, unknown 30, self 26, external 16, prod 12, audit 11, dallas 3, client 1). Every id below is cited under exactly one check (or under Not anticipable), so the counts add to 195. Where a defect sits on two mechanisms it is filed under the one whose check would have fired first on the diff that shipped it.

Lens placement up front: 14 of the 15 checks go to a NEW lens, `money-seam`, that runs on every tier A diff and whose first instruction is to read the row-truth rules (listed at the end). One check (14, siblings) goes to `consistency-check`, rewritten around "siblings and consumers of a fact" instead of columns and route mounts. None of the six checklists as written today names a money mechanism; the only money lines (SEC-38, SEC-39: "server re-computes, client totals are ignored") point the wrong way for this class, because in D0905 the server-side re-computation was the projection and the row was the truth.

## The shape of the defects

A proposal's money lives in at least six stores written by different code: the proposal row (`total_price`, `total_price_override`, `pricing_snapshot`, `amount_paid`, `status`, `payment_type`, `balance_due_date`), the `proposal_addons` rows, the invoice sub-ledger (`invoices`, `invoice_line_items`, `invoice_payments`), the payment ledger (`proposal_payments`, `proposal_refunds`, `tips`), payroll (`payouts`, payout lines, duty lines, clawbacks), and the client's rendered copy of all of it. No transaction owns a money fact end to end. Today 45 `UPDATE proposals` sites touch a money or status column, 8 webhook handler files settle money, 3 token-addressed submit paths write contract lines, 22 SQL sites mutate the roster, and five sweeps re-derive state. The defects are almost never wrong arithmetic. They are (a) a writer that moved one store and did not run, or conditionally ran, the step that moves the others (reprice without reconcile, roster change without re-accrual, refund without status re-derivation); (b) a reader that reconstructed a fact from a proxy instead of the stored truth (a label for "inside total_price", a live catalog row for a sold rate, `status` or `payment_type` for "paid", an amount match for a line's identity, worked hours for a contract quantity); (c) a re-derivation that assumed it owned every row it rebuilt and destroyed a hand-made or output-only fact; and (d) a second entrance (payment link, PATCH, v1 plan, dashboard refund, second client caller) that never got the tail its sibling had. The same ten or so seams recur from 2026-04 through 2026-10 in both directions (over- and under-billing, over- and under-refunding, over- and under-paying staff). The fleet found 96 of 195, which proves reviewers see these once pointed at the mechanism; the open ones cluster on the newest writers (`drinkPlans/submit.js` 16, `refundHelpers.js` 12, `invoiceLifecycle.js` 11, `lineItemCancel.js` 7, `payrollAccrual.js` 7).

6. **Credit what was captured; derive the flag.** Every credit to `proposals.amount_paid` is `amount_paid + <captured cents>` where captured is read from the Stripe object (`payment_intent.amount_received`, `session.amount_total`) and asserted equal to the figure the handler expected; never `amount_paid = total_price` or `= deposit_amount`.
